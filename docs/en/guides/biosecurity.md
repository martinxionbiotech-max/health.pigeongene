# Biosecurity for Racing Pigeons

Biosecurity is a systematic approach — a set of management measures — that lowers the risk of pathogens being "introduced" and "spread". It is the foundation of racing pigeon health management: the root of many disease outbreaks lies in a gap that has opened up in the biosecurity line.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Biosecurity is like the loft's **"security system"**, with one single goal: **keep germs outside the door**.

- **Isolation** = new arrivals are "screened and held" before being let in;
- **Disinfection** = the environment is "sterilised", leaving no corner untouched;
- **Control** = who and what goes in and out is restricted, reducing the risk of pathogens being carried in;
- **Monitoring** = "surveillance cameras" are set up, keeping you informed about pathogen activity at all times.

With security this strict, germs cannot get in.

## Core principles

The core of biosecurity is "prevention before it happens", lowering infection risk by blocking transmission routes:

- **Isolation**: newly introduced racing pigeons must be kept under observation and only mixed into the flock once confirmed healthy;
- **Disinfection**: disinfect lofts, equipment and transport crates on a regular schedule;
- **Control**: restrict the movement of people, vehicles and other poultry in and out of the loft;
- **Monitoring**: test for pathogens regularly to keep track of flock health.

## Routine management

- Keep the loft ventilated and dry, and control stocking density;
- Clean droppings promptly to reduce pathogen build-up;
- Keep drinking water and feed clean and free from contamination.

## Before a race and on introduction

- **Pre-race screening**: screen racing pigeons for viruses before a race, so that birds are not raced while carrying an infection;
- **Quarantine on introduction**: quarantining new pigeons before they are introduced is a key step in preventing pathogens from entering.

## Environmental monitoring

Regularly collect **environmental swabs** from loft floors, drinkers and feed troughs to assess the pathogen load in the environment. Environmental monitoring fills in the gaps left by individual sampling and reveals contamination that cannot be seen with the naked eye.

## Key points

- The core of biosecurity is "prevention before it happens", blocking transmission routes;
- Isolation, disinfection, control and monitoring are the four pillars;
- Pre-race screening and quarantine on introduction are the key to preventing pathogens from entering;
- Environmental swab monitoring can reveal contamination that individual sampling misses.
