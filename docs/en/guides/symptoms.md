# Racing Pigeon Symptom Index

Signs are warning signals of a racing pigeon's health. Remember, though: the same sign can be caused by several different diseases, signs alone cannot be used to confirm a diagnosis, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Signs are like the **"warning lights"** of disease — when a light comes on, something is wrong with the body. But a red or amber light does not tell you which part has failed, and many diseases switch on a similar light. So **lights only raise the alarm; testing pinpoints the cause**.

## Respiratory signs

Sneezing, nasal discharge, laboured breathing and other respiratory signs can be caused by several pathogens, including herpesvirus, Mycoplasma and Newcastle disease.

- Sneezing, nasal discharge
- Laboured breathing, open-mouth breathing
- Conjunctivitis, watery eyes

## Digestive signs

Diarrhoea, vomiting, tossing feed and other digestive signs may be caused by adenovirus, rotavirus, Salmonella and others.

- Watery diarrhoea, green droppings
- Vomiting, tossing feed
- Reduced appetite, weight loss

## Neurological signs

Head tilt, circling, drooping wings and other neurological signs are warning signals of serious diseases such as Newcastle disease.

- Head tilt, circling
- Drooping wings, unsteady gait
- Paralysis

## Skin and mucosal signs

Pox lesions, oral white plaques and similar lesions may be caused by pigeon pox, trichomonads, Candida and others.

- Skin pox lesions, scabs
- Oral white plaques, caseous necrosis
- Ruffled feathers

## Important reminder

If any suspicious sign appears, laboratory testing to confirm the pathogen is recommended, together with advice from a professional veterinarian, rather than self-medicating on signs alone.

## Quick-reference symptom chart

| Sign category | Common presentation | Directions to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Respiratory | Sneezing, nasal discharge, open-mouth breathing | Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, herpesvirus, Newcastle disease |
| Digestive | Green loose droppings, watery diarrhoea | Newcastle disease, adenovirus, Salmonella, rotavirus |
| Neurological | Head tilt, circling, ataxia | Newcastle disease, Salmonella (neurological form) |
| Oral | Yellow-white pseudomembrane, difficulty swallowing | Trichomonads, Candida, pigeon pox (mucosal form) |
| Skin | Pox lesions, scabs | Pigeon pox |

> The table above is only a reference for narrowing down possibilities and **cannot be used to confirm a diagnosis** — confirmation requires laboratory testing.

## Common misconceptions

- "A head tilt means Newcastle disease" — neurological Salmonella and other pathogens can also cause head tilt, so testing is needed to tell them apart;
- "Green droppings must mean a virus" — starvation and abnormal bile excretion can also produce green droppings, so other signs must be taken into account;
- "The signs have gone, so the bird is cured" — signs easing does not mean the pathogen has been cleared; the pigeon may become a carrier.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the first step after spotting signs?**

Isolate the suspected bird, observe and record how the signs change, and submit samples to confirm the pathogen, rather than medicating blindly on experience.

**What matters when recording signs?**

Record the number of birds affected, the timeline, the type of signs and any change of feed, as a reference for testing and veterinary diagnosis.
