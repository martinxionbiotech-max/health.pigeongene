# Pigeon Candida albicans Infection

Candida albicans is a common fungal pathogen of racing pigeons. This page describes its transmission routes, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language take (an analogy)

Candida albicans is the **"opportunist that moves in as soon as the door is left open"** — it usually coexists with the pigeon without causing trouble, but once antibiotics are misused or resistance drops, it multiplies heavily in the crop and oral cavity.

## Overview

Candida albicans is a common opportunistic fungal pathogen of the pigeon digestive tract; the crop and oral cavity are its favoured sites, and it is often linked to antibiotic misuse.

## Pathogen characteristics

Candida is a yeast-like fungus that can colonise normal pigeons in small numbers; it overgrows and causes disease when the microbial balance is disturbed or immunity declines.

## Transmission

Most infections are endogenous (overgrowth of the bird's own colonising flora), but the fungus can also spread through contaminated drinking water and equipment; squabs and sick or weakened pigeons are more susceptible.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Several diseases can produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Thickened crop wall with retained contents
- White pseudomembrane in the oral cavity
- Reduced appetite, weight loss
- Vomiting

## Impact

Digestion and absorption are affected, so birds lose weight and form declines; when mixed infection with trichomonads and other agents is present, the picture becomes more complex.

## Risk factors

Prolonged or indiscriminate antibiotic use, unclean drinking water, overcrowding, malnutrition and stress all raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: oral swab, crop contents
- **Test method**: microscopy / fungal culture
- **Report turnaround**: same-day for microscopy (fungal culture is counted separately).

Signs on their own are not the same as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than medicating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

It must be distinguished from trichomoniasis and the mucosal form of pigeon pox — the treatment for the three is completely different.

## Prevention

Use antibiotics rationally, keep drinking water clean, strengthen nutrition and control stress.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Disinfect drinkers, protect feed from mould, isolate sick pigeons and control stocking density.

## Value of testing

Identifies the cause of oral white plaques (trichomonads / Candida / pigeon pox) and prevents the wrong medication from making the condition worse.

## FAQ

**Is candidiasis caused by antibiotic misuse?**

Long-term antibiotic misuse that disrupts the microbial balance is an important trigger; rational use of antimicrobials is the key.

**Can it be cured completely?**

Proper antifungal treatment combined with better management can bring it under control, but relapse is common.
