# Pigeon Trichomoniasis (PiTG)

Trichomonas gallinae is a common parasitic pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission routes, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Pigeon trichomonads are the **"yellow cheese in the mouth"** — the typical lesion is a yellow-white, caseous material in the mouth and throat. Breeding pigeons pass the organism to their young while feeding them, and it is one of the most common parasites in a pigeon flock.

## Overview

T. gallinae lives in the mouth, throat and digestive tract of pigeons and is one of the most common protozoal pathogens of the species.

## Pathogen characteristics

The trichomonad is a single-celled protozoan spread through saliva and crop milk; feeding of the young by the breeding pair is the main route of transmission.

## Transmission routes

Transmission is mainly oral: feeding by the breeding pair, shared drinkers and direct contact all spread the organism, and contaminated drinkers are an important vehicle for spread within the flock.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Yellow-white caseous pseudomembranes in the mouth and throat
- Difficulty swallowing, reduced appetite
- Retention of crop contents
- Weight loss, laboured breathing (severe cases)

## Impact

The lesions interfere with feeding and swallowing, and the growth of young pigeons is held back; severe cases can cause death by suffocation, and infection before a race destroys racing condition.

## Risk factors

Shared drinkers that are not cleaned adequately, breeding stock carrying the organism, overcrowding and pre-race stress all raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: oral swab, crop lavage fluid
- **Test method**: real-time fluorescent PCR / microscopy
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days (microscopy is same-day).

Signs are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

White plaques in the mouth must be distinguished from candidiasis and the mucosal form of pigeon pox.

## Prevention

Change drinking water regularly, disinfect drinkers, carry out pre-race screening, follow a proper deworming protocol.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Wash drinkers daily, isolate affected pigeons, control stocking density, reduce stress.

## Value of testing

Confirms trichomoniasis and distinguishes it from other oral lesions such as candidiasis, guiding targeted medication and drinking-water management.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you tell trichomoniasis from candidiasis?**

They look similar and need microscopy or PCR to distinguish them; the medication used is completely different.

**Can it infect people?**

Pigeon trichomonads do not infect humans, so there is no need to worry.
