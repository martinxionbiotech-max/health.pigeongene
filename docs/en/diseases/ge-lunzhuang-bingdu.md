# Pigeon Rotavirus (PiRV)

Pigeon rotavirus (PiRV) is a common viral pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission routes, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Pigeon rotavirus is like a **"demolition crew that attacks the gut"** — it mainly causes intestinal damage and diarrhoea, young pigeons are the most susceptible, and the priorities in care are rehydration and preventing secondary infection.

## Overview

Rotavirus is one of the common causes of intestinal infection in young pigeons, producing diarrhoea, impaired digestion and absorption, and stunted growth.

## Pathogen characteristics

Rotavirus is a non-enveloped, segmented double-stranded RNA virus. It is relatively stable in the environment and spreads mainly by the faecal-oral route.

## Transmission routes

Spread by the faecal-oral route, with contaminated drinking water, feed and equipment as the main vehicles; transmission is rapid in densely housed flocks.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Watery diarrhoea
- Reduced appetite, dullness
- Dehydration, weight loss
- Stunted growth

## Impact

The main impact is on young pigeons: diarrhoea causes dehydration and impaired nutrient absorption, affecting growth and racing potential.

## Risk factors

Poor hygiene during the rearing period, contaminated drinking water, overcrowding and stress raise the risk of infection.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: cloacal swab, faeces
- **Test method**: real-time fluorescent RT-PCR
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Must be distinguished from diarrhoea caused by Salmonella, adenovirus and other pathogens.

## Prevention

Keep drinking water clean, strengthen disinfection of the rearing environment, reduce stress.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Clear faeces promptly, disinfect equipment, isolate pigeons with diarrhoea, control stocking density.

## Value of testing

Clarifies the cause of diarrhoea, distinguishes it from bacterial enteritis, and avoids the blind use of antibiotics.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does rotavirus need medication?**

Viral diarrhoea is managed mainly with fluid therapy, warmth and prevention of secondary infection; antibiotics have no effect on viruses.

**Which stage is mainly affected?**

Young pigeons are the most susceptible and show the most obvious signs.
