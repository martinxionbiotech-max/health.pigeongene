# Salmonella in Racing Pigeons

Salmonella spp. is a common bacterial pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language take (an analogy)

Salmonella is a **bacterium that causes trouble in many places** — the gut, joints, nerves and wings can all be affected, and because it is shared between pigeons and people, handling sick birds calls for strict hygiene.

## Overview

Salmonella (paratyphoid) is an important bacterial pathogen of pigeons. It can cause several disease forms involving the gut, joints, nervous system and reproductive tract, and it is zoonotic.

## Pathogen characteristics

Salmonella is a Gram-negative rod with strong survival in the environment; once feed and drinking water are contaminated, it can keep spreading for a long time.

## Transmission

Spread by the fecal-oral route, with contaminated feed, drinking water and equipment as the main vehicles; rodents and wild birds can carry and spread it, and it can also be transmitted vertically through the egg.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Diarrhea, green watery droppings
- Swollen joints, lameness
- Nervous signs, twisted head and neck
- Weight loss, reproductive problems

## Impact

Disease forms vary, and chronically infected carrier pigeons shed the organism continuously and contaminate the loft; people who handle sick birds or contaminated material need to pay attention to hand hygiene (zoonotic).

## Risk factors

Rodent infestation, contaminated feed and water, carrier birds brought in with new introductions, and poor hygiene all raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: cloacal swab, feces, joint fluid
- **Test method**: real-time PCR / bacterial culture
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days (culture-based testing is counted separately).

Signs on their own are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Must be differentiated from Newcastle disease (nervous signs), Mycoplasma (joint form) and other conditions.

## Prevention

Rodent and wild-bird control, safe feed and drinking water, quarantine and testing on introduction, and responsible use of medication guided by susceptibility testing.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Disinfect regularly, keep separate rearing areas, isolate sick pigeons, and wash and disinfect your hands after contact.

## Value of testing

Confirms carriers and affected individuals, guides susceptibility-based medication and clearance decisions, and reduces zoonotic risk.

## Common questions

**Can Salmonella spread to people?**

Yes. It is a zoonotic pathogen, so always wash and disinfect your hands after handling sick pigeons or contaminated material.

**Should chronic carriers be culled?**

It depends on the value of the pigeon and on veterinary assessment; carrier birds remain a continuous source of infection.
