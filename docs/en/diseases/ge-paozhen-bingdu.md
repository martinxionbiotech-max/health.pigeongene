# Pigeon Herpesvirus (PiHV)

Pigeon herpesvirus (PiHV) is a common viral pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission routes, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Pigeon herpesvirus is like a **"chronic cough hidden inside the body"** — it shows as respiratory signs during the acute phase, may stay latent for a long time after recovery, and is shed again when the bird is stressed.

## Overview

PiHV infects the respiratory tract of pigeons; signs are obvious in young birds, while adult pigeons are mostly subclinically infected and carry the virus long term.

## Pathogen characteristics

Herpesvirus is an enveloped DNA virus; after infection it can establish a latent infection that reactivates and is shed again when conditions are suitable.

## Transmission routes

Spread by respiratory droplets, direct contact and contaminated environments; carrier pigeons are a continuous source of infection.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Sneezing, nasal discharge
- Laboured breathing, inflammation of the throat
- Increased ocular discharge
- Dullness, reduced appetite

## Impact

Infection in young pigeons causes marked respiratory signs that affect growth and racing condition; carriers shed the virus again during stressful periods, putting the flock at risk.

## Risk factors

Stress from basketing and transport, overcrowding, poor ventilation and mixing of groups raise the risk of relapse and spread.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: throat swab, conjunctival swab
- **Test method**: real-time fluorescent PCR (qPCR)
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Must be distinguished from respiratory signs caused by Mycoplasma, Chlamydia and other pathogens.

## Prevention

Reduce stress, improve ventilation, isolate affected pigeons, control stocking density.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Disinfect before and after basketing, quarantine and observe new pigeons, disinfect the environment regularly.

## Value of testing

Investigates the cause of respiratory signs, distinguishes herpesvirus from Mycoplasma and Chlamydia infection, and guides precise medication.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can herpesvirus be cured completely?**

Most recovered pigeons become latent carriers; the aim of management is to control relapses and reduce virus shedding.

**Can it infect people?**

Pigeon herpesvirus is host-specific and does not infect humans.
