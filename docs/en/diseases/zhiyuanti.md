# Mycoplasma in Racing Pigeons

Mycoplasma spp. is a common bacterial pathogen of racing pigeons. This page covers transmission, signs, diagnosis, control and prevention points.

## In Plain Terms (An Analogy)

Mycoplasma is like **a lingering respiratory complaint that keeps dragging on** — a single infection is not fatal, but it flares up again and again and never fully clears, making it one of the main culprits behind a flock's suboptimal respiratory health.

## Overview

Mycoplasma infects the respiratory tract of pigeons. The course is protracted and relapse after stress is common; it is the main pathogen of chronic respiratory disease in racing pigeons.

## Pathogen Characteristics

Mycoplasma is a prokaryotic microorganism without a cell wall. It is naturally resistant to several commonly used antibiotics (such as the penicillins), so treatment has to use drugs that target it specifically.

## Transmission

Spread by respiratory droplets and direct contact; it can also pass vertically through the egg, with carrier breeding pigeons spreading it to young birds.

## Common Signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Several diseases produce similar signs; laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Nasal discharge, sneezing
- Swollen eyelids, watery eyes
- Respiratory rales
- Chronic weight loss, declining condition

## Impact

The course becomes chronic. Repeated flare-ups burn through the bird's reserves, affecting training and racing condition and breeding; mixed infections make the disease worse.

## Risk Factors

Mixing birds in the basket at race marking, poor ventilation, high ammonia levels, stress and overcrowding all raise the risk of relapse.

## Diagnosis and Testing

- **Sample types**: throat swabs, conjunctival swabs
- **Test method**: real-time PCR
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs on their own are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential Diagnosis

Must be distinguished from respiratory signs caused by Chlamydia, herpesvirus and other agents.

## Prevention

Improve ventilation, lower ammonia, reduce stress, and use medication sensibly (complete the full course).

## Biosecurity Advice

Quarantine and test new arrivals, disinfect regularly, control density, and observe birds after race marking.

## Testing Value

Identifies the respiratory pathogen, separates it from Chlamydia, and guides targeted medication and course management.

## FAQ

**Can Mycoplasma be cured for good?**

Proper treatment controls the signs, but relapse after stress is common; management matters more than medication.

**Why does penicillin not work?**

Mycoplasma has no cell wall, so drugs that act on the cell wall, such as the penicillins, have no effect.
