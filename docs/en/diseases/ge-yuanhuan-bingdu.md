# Pigeon Circovirus (PiCV)

Pigeon Circovirus (PiCV) is a common viral pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language take (an analogy)

Pigeon Circovirus is like an **immune-system troublemaker** — its own signs are unremarkable, yet it erodes the immunity of young pigeons and opens the door for other pathogens.

## Overview

Pigeon Circovirus (PiCV) infects lymphocytes and suppresses immune function; it is an important pathogen associated with young pigeon disease syndrome.

## Pathogen characteristics

Circoviruses are among the smallest animal viruses: non-enveloped DNA viruses with strong resistance in the environment, and infected pigeons can carry and shed the virus over the long term.

## Transmission

Spread by the fecal-oral route, direct contact and contaminated environments; breeder pigeons carrying the virus are the main source of infection for young birds.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Stunted growth and weight loss in young pigeons
- Poor feather development
- Reduced immune function and more secondary infections
- Poor appetite and listlessness

## Impact

Immunosuppression makes young pigeons more vulnerable to secondary viral, bacterial and parasite infections — a hidden driver of mixed losses during the rearing period.

## Risk factors

Carrier breeder pigeons, poor hygiene during rearing, overcrowding and malnutrition all raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: cloacal swab, blood, tissue
- **Test method**: real-time PCR (qPCR)
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs on their own are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Young pigeon syndrome requires joint screening for adenovirus, polyomavirus and other pathogens as well.

## Prevention

Quarantine and testing on introduction, better nutrition and management during rearing, and control of secondary infections.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Quarantine new arrivals, disinfect regularly, and isolate thin young pigeons promptly.

## Value of testing

Assesses the risk of immunosuppression in the loft and guides rearing management and combined control plans.

## Common questions

**Is there a vaccine against circovirus?**

There is no commercial vaccine at present; control relies mainly on quarantine and testing, management and nutritional support.

**Should infected pigeons be culled?**

It depends on the state of the loft; a clearance strategy is best decided together with testing and veterinary assessment.
