# Chlamydia psittaci in Racing Pigeons

Chlamydia psittaci is a common bacterial pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language take (an analogy)

Chlamydia psittaci is a **respiratory pathogen that both pigeons and people need protection from** — pigeons show conjunctivitis and respiratory signs, people can be infected as well, and control has to work on both fronts.

## Overview

Chlamydia psittaci (C. psittaci) infects the respiratory tract and conjunctiva of pigeons and is commonly known as ornithosis; it is an important zoonotic pathogen.

## Pathogen characteristics

Chlamydiae are obligate intracellular microorganisms that are shed in respiratory secretions and feces and, once dry, spread with dust.

## Transmission

Spread by inhaling dust containing the organism and by direct contact with secretions; dust raised while cleaning the loft is an important transmission scenario.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Conjunctivitis in one or both eyes, watery eyes
- Sneezing, increased nasal discharge
- Difficulty breathing
- Depression, weight loss

## Impact

Infection affects racing form and reproduction; sick pigeons shed the organism and contaminate the environment, and keepers face a risk of infection (zoonotic).

## Risk factors

Poor loft ventilation, heavy dust, overcrowding and cleaning without protective equipment all raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: conjunctival swab, tracheal swab, feces
- **Test method**: real-time PCR
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs on their own are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Must be differentiated from respiratory and ocular signs caused by Mycoplasma, herpesvirus and other pathogens.

## Prevention

Improve ventilation, use wet cleaning to reduce dust, isolate sick pigeons, and treat according to proper protocols.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Wear a mask while cleaning, disinfect regularly, control density, and reduce dust.

## Value of testing

Confirms chlamydial infection and differentiates it from Mycoplasma and other pathogens, guides appropriate medication, and reminds keepers to use personal protection.

## Common questions

**Can people become infected with Chlamydia psittaci?**

Yes. Keepers should wear a mask when cleaning the loft, and if fever or other symptoms appear, seek medical care promptly and mention the history of contact.

**How is it distinguished from Mycoplasma infection?**

The signs are similar, so laboratory testing is needed to tell them apart, and the treatment plans differ.
