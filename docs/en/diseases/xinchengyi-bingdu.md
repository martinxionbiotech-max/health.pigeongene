# Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV) in Pigeons

Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV) is a common viral pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language take (an analogy)

Newcastle disease is like a **virulent infectious disease of the loft** — once it breaks out, the nervous, respiratory and digestive systems are all affected, and it is a top-priority threat that requires close prevention and handling under official rules.

## Overview

Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV) belongs to the family Paramyxoviridae as avian paramyxovirus type 1 (APMV-1). It is an important pathogen of birds and can cause nervous, respiratory and digestive signs in racing pigeons; it is a notifiable disease.

## Pathogen characteristics

NDV is an enveloped, single-stranded negative-sense RNA virus. Virulence varies widely between strains: virulent strains are strongly pathogenic and spread rapidly, and infection of a loft with a virulent strain can cause serious losses.

## Transmission

Spread mainly through the respiratory and digestive routes and by direct contact. The virus can spread through air, droplets, feces, contaminated equipment and people carrying it; stressful situations such as basketing and transport accelerate spread.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Nervous signs, twisted neck, circling
- Difficulty breathing, open-mouth breathing
- Green watery droppings
- Complete loss of appetite, depression
- Drop in egg production, high mortality

## Impact

Virulent infection can cause nervous signs, difficulty breathing and green watery droppings with very high mortality, and an outbreak can cause major losses in a loft; because the disease is notifiable, outbreak response involves mandatory measures.

## Risk factors

Overcrowding, poor hygiene, gaps in immunity, stress from basketing and transport, and contact with wild birds or other poultry all raise the risk of infection and spread.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: cloacal swab, tracheal swab, tissue
- **Test method**: real-time RT-PCR
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs on their own are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Must be differentiated from nervous or digestive disease caused by adenovirus, Salmonella and other pathogens; Newcastle disease is characterized by nervous signs, difficulty breathing, green watery droppings and high mortality.

## Prevention

Vaccinate according to a proper schedule and monitor antibody levels; strengthen biosecurity; report suspected outbreaks as required and apply isolation and control measures.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Improve loft hygiene, control stocking density, disinfect regularly, isolate new arrivals and sick pigeons, and reduce basketing and transport stress.

## Value of testing

Rapidly confirms Newcastle disease and differentiates it from adenovirus, Salmonella and other pathogens, guides emergency vaccination and isolation, and is a core test for loft disease control and quarantine on introduction.

## Common questions

**Does the presence of signs mean the pigeon is infected with Newcastle Disease Virus?**

Not necessarily. Signs can be caused by several pathogens or by non-infectious factors, so laboratory testing is needed for confirmation.

**When should testing be considered?**

Testing can be considered when the loft shows suspicious signs, before a race, when new pigeons are introduced, or when an outbreak occurs nearby.

**Can Newcastle disease be treated?**

There is no specific antiviral drug. Control relies mainly on vaccination and biosecurity, and confirmed cases are handled according to regulations.
