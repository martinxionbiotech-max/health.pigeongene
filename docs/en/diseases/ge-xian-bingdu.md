# Pigeon Adenovirus (PiADV)

Pigeon adenovirus (PiADV) is a common viral pathogen in racing pigeons. This page covers its transmission routes, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language explanation (an analogy)

Pigeon adenovirus comes in two **"brothers"** — **type I troubles the gut, type II damages the liver** — and it is a common cause of young pigeon disease and of vomiting and diarrhoea during training and racing.

## Overview

Pigeon adenovirus has two types: type I mainly causes digestive disease in young pigeons, and type II can cause necrosis of the liver. Both are common pathogens during training and racing.

## Pathogen characteristics

Adenovirus is a non-enveloped DNA virus, stable in the environment and spread mainly by the faecal-oral route.

## Transmission routes

Spread by the faecal-oral route, contaminated feed and water and direct contact; mixed baskets at basketing and the stress of training and racing are important transmission situations.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Many diseases produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Vomiting, diarrhoea (type I)
- Complete loss of appetite, dullness
- Signs of liver damage (type II)
- Stunted growth and sudden death in young pigeons

## Impact

An outbreak during training and racing can pull down the condition of the whole team; type II infection damages the liver and can be fatal in severe cases.

## Risk factors

Frequent basketing for training and racing, contact between mixed groups, stress and poor hygiene raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: cloacal swab, faeces, tissue
- **Test method**: real-time fluorescent PCR (qPCR)
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs are not the same thing as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than treating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Must be distinguished from digestive signs caused by pigeon circovirus, Newcastle disease, Salmonella and other pathogens.

## Prevention

Reduce stress during training and racing, support nutrition, isolate affected pigeons, keep drinking water clean.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Quarantine and observe pigeons returning from basketing, disinfect equipment, clear vomit and faeces promptly.

## Value of testing

Separates viral from bacterial digestive disease, avoids overuse of antibiotics, and informs adjustment of the training and racing schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is vomiting during training and racing always adenovirus?**

Not necessarily. Vomiting and diarrhoea can have many causes and need laboratory testing for confirmation.

**How are type I and type II distinguished?**

By combining pathogen testing with clinical findings; with type II infection, liver damage needs particular attention.
