# Pigeon Polyomavirus in Racing Pigeons

Pigeon Polyomavirus (PiAPV) is a common viral pathogen of racing pigeons. This page describes its transmission routes, signs, diagnosis and control points.

## Plain-language take (an analogy)

Pigeon Polyomavirus is a **"high-risk virus that picks on young pigeons"** — mortality after infection is high and the course is acute, which makes it a pathogen that must be screened for when introducing birds and rearing squabs.

## Overview

Pigeon Polyomavirus (PiAPV) is an important viral pathogen of pigeons, mainly harming young birds and capable of causing acute death, while adult pigeons are mostly silent carriers.

## Pathogen characteristics

Polyomaviruses are non-enveloped DNA viruses with fairly strong resistance to physical and chemical factors and long survival in the environment.

## Transmission

Spread by the faecal-oral route, direct contact and contaminated environments; silently infected breeding pigeons can transmit the virus vertically, which makes them a key target in the quarantine testing of introduced birds.

## Common signs

**Note**: the signs below **cannot be used on their own to confirm a diagnosis**. Several diseases can produce similar signs, and laboratory testing is the basis for confirmation.

- Acute death in young pigeons
- Reduced appetite, listlessness
- Digestive disturbance
- Poor feather development

## Impact

Mortality among infected young pigeons is high, and an outbreak can cause major losses in a rearing batch; carrier breeding pigeons shed virus continuously, threatening the long-term health of the flock.

## Risk factors

Introducing birds without quarantine testing, overcrowding during the rearing period and weak hygiene management all raise the risk.

## Diagnosis and testing

- **Sample types**: cloacal swab, tissue
- **Test method**: probe-based real-time PCR (qPCR)
- **Report turnaround**: 2-3 working days.

Signs on their own are not the same as disease. When suspicious signs appear, confirm the pathogen through laboratory testing rather than medicating on signs alone.

## Differential diagnosis

Acute death in young pigeons must be distinguished from the young pigeon disease caused by Pigeon Circovirus, Adenovirus and similar agents.

## Prevention

Quarantine testing of introduced birds, culling of breeding pigeons that shed virus continuously, and stronger hygiene management during the rearing period.

## Biosecurity recommendations

Quarantine and test new pigeons, disinfect the rearing environment regularly, control density and remove droppings promptly.

## Value of testing

Screens carrier breeding pigeons and confirms the cause of acute death in young pigeons, supporting eradication decisions and introductions.

## FAQ

**Do adult pigeons need testing?**

Testing is recommended. Adult pigeons often carry the virus silently and spread it, so quarantine testing of newly introduced birds is especially necessary.

**Is there a vaccine?**

Commercial vaccines are limited, so control relies mainly on culling carriers through testing together with biosecurity.
