# Racing Pigeon Health Glossary

| Term | English | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Pathogen | Pathogen | A microorganism or parasite that causes disease, including viruses, bacteria, fungi and parasites. |
| Virus | Virus | A type of non-cellular microorganism that has to replicate inside a host cell; the cause of several racing pigeon diseases. |
| Bacteria | Bacteria | Single-celled prokaryotic microorganisms; some bacteria can cause disease, such as Salmonella spp. |
| Fungus | Fungus | A eukaryotic microorganism; some fungi can cause disease, such as Candida albicans. |
| Parasite | Parasite | An organism that lives inside or on the surface of a host and depends on it for nutrients, such as trichomonads. |
| PCR | Polymerase Chain Reaction | Polymerase chain reaction: a technique for amplifying a specific DNA fragment in vitro. |
| qPCR | Real-time Quantitative PCR | Real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR: quantifying and analysing a target nucleic acid in real time through a fluorescent signal. |
| RT-PCR | Reverse Transcription PCR | Reverse transcription PCR: RNA is first reverse transcribed into cDNA and then amplified; used to detect RNA viruses. |
| Biosecurity | Biosecurity | A systematic approach that reduces the risk of pathogen spread through isolation, disinfection, control and monitoring. |
| Differential diagnosis | Differential Diagnosis | The process of distinguishing the true cause from several possible causes with similar signs, by testing and judgement. |
| Latent infection | Latent Infection | Infection in which the pathogen does not cause disease immediately but lies dormant in the body, reactivating under conditions such as stress. |
| Immunosuppression | Immunosuppression | A state in which immune system function is suppressed, leaving the body more susceptible to pathogens. |
| Aerosol | Aerosol | Fine particles or droplets suspended in the air, which can act as a vehicle for pathogen transmission. |
| Fecal-oral route | Fecal-Oral Route | A transmission route in which the pathogen is shed in faeces and reaches another host through contaminated food, water or contact. |
| Internal control | Internal Control | A control marker in a test system, used to monitor whether nucleic acid extraction and amplification are working normally. |
| UNG | Uracil-N-Glycosylase | An enzyme used to degrade previous amplification products containing dUTP, preventing false positives caused by PCR contamination. |
| Sensitivity | Sensitivity | The ability of a test method to detect a target substance at low concentration. |
| Specificity | Specificity | The ability of a test method to identify the target accurately and avoid cross-reaction with other substances. |
| Swab | Swab | A sterile sampling tool used to collect samples (cloaca, throat, and so on). |
| Quarantine | Quarantine | A measure in which newly introduced or suspected-infected individuals are isolated and observed separately to prevent pathogen spread. |
| Inactivated vaccine | Inactivated Vaccine | A vaccine made by inactivating the pathogen; it stimulates immunity but does not cause disease. |
| Maternal antibody | Maternal Antibody | Antibody that a young bird receives from its mother, providing early passive immune protection. |
| Vaccination schedule | Vaccination Schedule | A vaccination plan drawn up according to age, season and epidemiology. |
| Secondary infection | Secondary Infection | Infection caused by opportunistic pathogens once the body's immunity has fallen. |
| Aerosol | Aerosol | Fine droplets or particles suspended in the air; one of the routes by which pathogens spread. |
| Biosecurity | Biosecurity | Systematic management measures that reduce the risk of pathogens being introduced and spreading. |
